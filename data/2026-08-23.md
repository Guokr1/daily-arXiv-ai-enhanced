TL;DR: 本研究利用硅纳米线场效应晶体管（SiNWFET）传感器实时监测大肠杆菌对氨苄青霉素（AMP）的代谢响应，发现AMP处理引发双相胞外pH信号（先酸化后碱化），并揭示甲酸代谢是AMP杀菌活性相关ATP升高的关键通路，且与传统观点不同，NADH/NADPH变化不支持氧化应激作为主要杀菌机制。

Motivation: 抗生素杀菌常归因于对特定细胞靶点的抑制，但代谢过程可强烈影响药物疗效，然而代谢响应与抗生素致死性之间的关系仍未被充分理解。

Method: 采用SiNWFET传感器实时监测大肠杆菌对AMP的代谢响应；结合代谢组学分析胞外代谢物变化；利用代谢和呼吸突变体验证代谢通路重编程；分析ATP动态、甲酸分泌与细菌杀伤的关联。

Result: AMP处理诱导双相pH信号（快速酸化后碱化）；酸化源于有机酸分泌，碱化与氨基酸代谢改变和甲酸通量相关；AMP致死性与ATP动态和甲酸分泌强相关；反驳了氧化应激作为主要杀菌机制的传统观点。

Conclusion: 甲酸代谢是AMP杀菌活性中ATP升高的关键通路；SiNWFET传感器可作为多功能的免标记工具，用于探明抗生素机制、快速评估细菌敏感性并指导抗菌疗法开发。

Abstract: Antibiotic killing is often attributed to inhibition of specific cellular targets, yet metabolic processes can strongly influence drug efficacy. However, the relationship between metabolic responses and antibiotic lethality remains incompletely understood. Here, we employed silicon nanowire field-effect transistor (SiNWFET) sensors to monitor real-time metabolic responses of Escherichia coli to ampicillin (AMP). AMP treatment induced a biphasic extracellular pH signature, characterized by rapid acidification followed by alkalization. Metabolomic analyses revealed that the initial acidification resulted from organic acid secretion, whereas the subsequent alkalization was associated with altered amino acid metabolism and formate flux. Using metabolic and respiratory mutants, we found that these extracellular signatures reflected pathway-specific metabolic rewiring that predicted bacterial killing. AMP lethality strongly correlated with ATP dynamics and formate secretion: strains exhibiting larger AMP-induced ATP increases and greater formate secretion showed enhanced susceptibility. In contrast to literature, changes in NADH and NADPH levels did not support redox stress as the primary bacterial-killing mechanism. Together, our findings identify formate metabolism as a key pathway underlying the elevated ATP levels associated with AMP bactericidal activity. These results demonstrate that SiNWFET sensors provide a versatile label-free tool for probing antibiotic mechanisms, rapidly assessing bacterial susceptibility, and potentially guiding antimicrobial therapy development.
